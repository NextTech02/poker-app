How the structure is calculated

The calculator starts from two ends. The first is the starting blind: the stack begins at about 100 big blinds, or fewer if the smallest chip doesn’t allow it. The second is the final blind, chosen so that by heads-up the average stack is close to 20 big blinds.

Between those two ends, each level rises by the same proportion. Each value is then rounded to a number the chips on the table can pay: the small blind is always half the big blind and a multiple of the smallest chip.

The length is the goal you set in the “Target length” field. The actual tournament depends on how many players show up and how they play, so use the table as a starting point and adjust it to your crowd.

Frequently asked questions

How do I download the structure as a spreadsheet?

Click “Download spreadsheet”. The file opens in Excel and Google Sheets, already filled in with levels, blinds, ante, minutes and start times. The “Print” button produces a sheet with just the table.

What is the smallest chip?

It’s the lowest chip value that will be in play. If your smallest chip is 25, the small blind has to be a multiple of 25, so the calculator never suggests a 110/220 level.

Should I use an ante?

It’s optional. The most common format today is the big blind ante, where only the big blind pays the table’s ante. It makes the game faster than collecting from every player. Enter 0 to leave it out.

How do I use this structure in the timer?

Click “Open in the timer”. The structure goes to the free poker tournament timer, ready to run on the TV.
