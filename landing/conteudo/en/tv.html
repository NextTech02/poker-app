It’s the screen players look at most during the night, and the one that draws the most complaints when it freezes. In AceBlind, it comes straight from the tournament: whatever the tournament director changes shows up on the TV, with nobody retyping anything.

What’s on the screen

  • Level clock and an audible alert in the final seconds
  • Current blinds and ante, plus the next level
  • Players remaining and average stack
  • Prize pool, guarantee and prizes

How to put it on the TV

Each tournament day has a code. Open the address in the TV’s browser, or on a laptop connected to it, type the code and you’re done. The screen doesn’t ask for a login, so no club password is left exposed on the floor.

With your club’s look

You can change the colors, background and logo, and add an image or video to the board. It’s a good spot for the event sponsor or for promoting your next tournament.

Tables and seats screen

Besides the clock, there’s a public tables and seats screen. It shows where each player is seated, which clears the line at the front desk right after the draw.

Frequently asked questions

Does the TV screen need special equipment?

No. Any TV or monitor with a browser gets in with the code. It also works with a laptop connected to the TV by HDMI cable.

Can I have more than one TV on the floor?

Yes. Open the same code on each screen: they all show the same tournament.

What happens to the TV if the internet drops?

A screen that was already open keeps counting. Changes the director makes during the outage don’t reach the TV until the connection returns, and each device runs on its own clock. If the TV is reloaded without a network, it may lose the clock, so don’t refresh the page at that point.
